SCHOOLS IN VICTORIA · FINAL VERSION

Methods & design decisions

Yousif Gaggi · 27 October 2026. This document explains the data, choices and limits behind the page.

Domain, audience and purpose

Domain: school education in Victoria. Who: a general Australian audience interested in how the school system varies across places and sectors. Why: school counts alone can mislead when schools differ in size, year levels and surrounding population. The page moves from the statewide network and its historical growth to school type, regional composition, enrolment and population. It ends with quantified future youth-population change and its implications for community needs. It is not a school recommendation or a capacity assessment.

What: sources and observation dates

  • Victorian Department of Education, School Locations 2026: registered school location, sector, type and LGA. Locations refer to school records and do not enumerate every campus.
  • Department of Education, February 2026 FTE enrolments: census-based FTE totals and year levels.
  • ABS, Regional population by age and sex, 2025: Table 3, persons by age and LGA at 30 June 2025. The release date is 27 August 2026; the observations are 2025.
  • ABS 2025 LGA boundaries: geographic context, simplified for browser display.
  • Department of Education, Schools and enrolments 2026 workbook: historical school counts. Only rows for 2017–2026 are retained; the chart uses the 2017 and 2026 endpoints. Publication year and observation year are different.

School-level and projection files were retrieved on 27 October 2026; ABS files were retrieved on 12 October 2026. All release pages were checked on 27 October 2026. The latest school-level files are 2026; the latest ABS LGA age estimates are June 2025. Department datasets are CC BY 4.0; ABS material is attributed to the Commonwealth of Australia.

Preparation and checks

  • School locations and enrolments are joined one-to-one on sector plus school number. School number alone is not unique across sectors. The join produces 2,319 school records.
  • FTE reconciles to 1,070,240.3: Government 665,540.1; Catholic 221,143.0; Independent 183,557.2. School counts are 1,593, 494 and 232 respectively.
  • LGA names are normalised and matched to ABS codes. Population ages 5–9, 10–14 and 15–19 are summed to 1,256,463 across 80 areas, including Unincorporated Victoria.
  • “Mainstream school records” includes Primary, Secondary and Pri/Sec, excluding Virtual School Victoria: 2,194 records. These records underpin the stage comparison and population comparisons.
  • Two zero-FTE school records remain in school counts but are excluded from the size-distribution chart. The size chart also excludes non-mainstream and virtual records.
  • Metro/Non Metro is the Department’s LGA_TYPE field. One Unknown record is excluded only from that comparison. Region totals reconcile to 1,403 and 915.
  • A combined school may count in both primary-stage and secondary-stage totals if it reports positive enrolments at each stage.
  • Boundary geometry is simplified to reduce download size; two null-geometry special codes are excluded. Eighty spatial features remain. Geometry simplification does not alter statistical totals.
  • The 2026 location file has 2,320 rows but 2,319 unique sector–school keys. Victory Christian College (Independent 1946) appears at two coordinates with the same address. We retain the Strathdale coordinate (144.313456, −36.782625), consistent with the 2025 record and registered main campus at 6 Kairn Road. The second location is not counted as another school and whole-school FTE is never duplicated. VRQA register.
  • The historical summary lists 233 Independent schools in 2026, while the latest location and FTE extracts contain 232 unique Independent schools. The growth chart retains the official historical series; composition and geographic charts consistently use the school-level extracts. This one-record discrepancy is stated in the growth caption.

Geographic assignment and coverage

The 80 areas comprise 79 council areas plus Unincorporated Victoria. School-level FTE is summed using the LGA in each school’s location record. A school’s entire enrolment is assigned to that LGA; the data do not distribute enrolments between campuses. Consequently these figures describe school-record geography, not the actual location of every classroom or student. Cross-boundary campuses and student travel can both affect the comparison with resident population.

Population measure

Young residents per school = residents aged 5–19 ÷ mainstream school records in the LGA. Class boundaries are 250, 500, 750 and 1,000 residents per school record. Regular 250-person intervals make adjacent classes easy to interpret, with an open-ended top class. These are descriptive design choices, not policy thresholds. The same breaks apply in both map views. Darker brown means a higher ratio. There are no zero-school LGAs in this extract; a zero-school denominator would be null/undefined and must never be displayed as a zero ratio.

The earlier schools-per-1,000 measure is mathematically valid, but the reciprocal better fits the narrative about how many young residents correspond to each school record. It does not measure enrolment load, capacity, class size or shortage. Ages 5–19 include non-students, and students can cross LGA boundaries.

Visual design and interaction

Sector hue is consistent: Government muted blue, Catholic terracotta and Independent teal. A sequential light-to-dark brown scale encodes numerical magnitude; brown is also used for the page framing and headings. Each quantitative chart has its own labelled units and scale. Government blue, Catholic terracotta and Independent teal remain categorical sector identifiers. Stage comparisons use charcoal/grey and circle/square shapes. A dark opening and conclusion frame light chart panels, with aligned columns and repeated spacing. Libre Baskerville headings and DM Sans body text are bundled locally with their SIL Open Font Licence files.

Map-area controls, sector/type filters, the school-size filter and LGA highlighting serve specific comparison tasks. Tooltips provide exact values. Expandable tables support precise sector, school-type and enrolment comparisons without hover. The population selector also provides a keyboard-accessible numeric summary and a CSV download. The page remains a single scrolling narrative; controls never replace entire sections. Readable Vega-Lite specifications are in charts/; the hierarchical treemap uses Vega.

Map projection and interpretation

The point and choropleth maps use Mercator to retain recognisable local shapes over Victoria. It is not an equal-area projection: polygon size must not be read as population. The separate cartogram uses circle area for population and explicitly sacrifices exact position. The choropleth uses population per school record rather than raw population counts, with fixed descriptive classes shared between map views.

Cartogram construction

Population is encoded by circle area, not radius. Starting positions use the centroid of each LGA’s largest polygon. Deterministic collision relaxation displaces circles while attracting them toward those positions. Circle sizes scale with the plotting area. Displacement means directions and distances are approximate, and circles are not geographic boundaries.

Projected population and community needs

Victoria in Future 2023, Release 2 is the latest official LGA age projection available at the check date. The workbook’s ERP_by_Age_Persons sheet provides LGA totals separately from VIFSA subdivisions; only the 80 LGA total rows are used. For each endpoint, the 5–9, 10–14 and 15–19 bands are summed. Changes are calculated before rounding. The LGA totals reconcile to the workbook’s Victoria row. Merri-bek is matched by its name because its LGA code differs between the projection and 2025 ABS releases.

The projected youth population rises from approximately 1,280,456 in 2026 to 1,385,189 in 2036, an increase of about 104,732. Wyndham, Melton and Casey contribute about 58,558 of that net increase (56%). Net statewide change includes declines elsewhere. Both chart endpoints are projections from the same release; they are not mixed with the observed 2025 ABS figures. The projection workbook is based on earlier demographic assumptions and is not a prediction of the exact outcome.

Interpretation limits and submission checks

School-location records describe 2026 registrations, enrolment is the February 2026 census, and resident population is estimated at June 2025. Using the latest release for each measure creates a date mismatch that is disclosed alongside the population comparisons. It is not a same-date capacity measure. Resident ages, school students, campus geography and cross-boundary travel also differ.

Community needs are examined through measured school provision and projected youth growth. School capacity, planned places, journey times, fees, disability support and language-support demand are not measured here. Consequently the story identifies planning priorities rather than declaring shortages or how many new schools are required. The final hand-drawn A4 sketch and Moodle submission requirements remain separate from this webpage.
