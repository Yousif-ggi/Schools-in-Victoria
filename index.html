THE GEOGRAPHY OF SCHOOLING

A school system spread across the state.

A LOCATION IS ONLY THE BEGINNING

More schools does not always mean more places.

A small rural school and a large suburban school each count as one. Enrolments add a second perspective, while population helps put those counts in context.

Throughout this page, “FTE” means full-time-equivalent enrolment. Two half-time enrolments add up to one FTE.

The mix matters.

Sectors differ in the types of schools they operate. Comparing composition, rather than just totals, reveals those differences more clearly.

Schools come in very different sizes.

Enrolment shows how many full-time-equivalent students attend, rather than how many schools exist. Compare like school types, then look at how students are distributed across the system.

A CLOSER LOOK AT WYNDHAM

Young residents, served by 68 mainstream physical school records.

That is 0.85 schools per 1,000 residents aged 5–19. Local schools report 64,621 FTE enrolments. These measures describe different things: where young people live and where students enrol.

Three perspectives. A more complete picture.

Locations show where schools are. Enrolments show their scale. Resident population provides context. Together, they help us ask better questions about provision—while leaving capacity, travel and families’ choices open for further investigation.

Read these comparisons carefully

Schools are joined by sector and school number. LGA names are matched to ABS codes. Ages 5–19 are the sum of three published age bands; they are an approximate youth denominator, not an exact school-age population.

“Mainstream physical” excludes Special and Language school types and Virtual School Victoria: 2,178 records. School locations do not enumerate every campus. Two zero-enrolment records remain in school counts but are excluded from the size distribution.

Boundary geometry is simplified for display. FTE measures enrolment, not capacity. Read methodology and checks ↗
